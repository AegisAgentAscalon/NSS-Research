**The completed main result is a fresh v0.6 capture, score and exact replay under the reviewed CM-02 scientific design, with an explicitly inherited v0.5 fixture.** It is neither a clean first attempt nor a relabeling of an earlier complete main run. `REPORT.md` and `STATUS.json` identify 32 chronological sequences, 192 query occasions, five fixed near-reader seeds, zero training and unchanged Q010 exports. ## Version and source boundary

The reviewed `protocol/REVIEW.md` introduced a separately costed non-neural **X_latest** control, which follows an observed revision chain to current content. That was part of reviewed scientific protocol v0.2, not a result-driven v0.6 change. The v0.3 execution specification fixed wire fields, two task slots, visible row registration, archive closure, exact single-stage model inputs, controls and resource rules **before generated fixture or main stimuli** (`protocol/EXECUTION_NOTES.md`, `PROTOCOL_v0.3.json`). It declared no change to reviewed arms, event schedule, seeds, policies or thresholds. This matters at F:16: W/I's intentional refusal of a stale revision is a known-answer recall loss under their pinned policy; X_latest's 5/5 recovery of the current answer uses a different, declared policy and extra revision index, not an improved neural reader.

The v0.3 fixture capture used 45 model calls, then the scorer crashed by indexing a reference absent on O's original-query route. No main inputs were generated. The retained v0.4 diff adds a null-reference guard to the scorer, a regression test and global attempt-ledger inclusion in `src/run_stage.py`; no model, generator, controller, event template, seed or threshold changed (`protocol/REPAIR_v0.4.md`, nested v0.3/v0.4 `SOURCE_LOCK.json`). That scorer repair preceded the successful v0.4 fixture and any main result. v0.4 then timed out during main capture before scoring. v0.5 repeated fixture/main with unchanged scientific implementation but suffered the same command transport interruption. Source-lock comparison shows v0.4–v0.6 baseline hashes equal and scientific source hashes equal; only version/counter freeze code and transport/protocol records differ (`protocol/TRANSPORT_RECOVERY_v0.5.md`, `TRANSPORT_RECOVERY_v0.6.md`, source locks). The package states no completed main score existed before v0.6 and no seed/case/criterion selection followed the interruptions. Partial arrays and failures remain available, so this review relies on the documented chronology and static source identities rather than claiming proof of a blinded process.

v0.6 reused the complete v0.5 fixture after byte-identical scientific-source checks, independent rescoring (16 files identical) and its already completed fresh-process fixture replay (360 arrays). It reports **zero new v0.6 fixture model calls**, an explicit carry-forward exception, not a fresh fixture rerun (`audit/MAIN_DISPATCH_GATE.json`, `INHERITED_FIXTURE_SOURCE_IDENTITY.json`). v0.6 then regenerated and scored the entire main study and replayed it in new processes: 1,800 capture and 1,800 replay model calls; the report claims 14,400 exactly matching arrays and 16 identical independent main score files. `audit/UNCHANGED_RECOVERY_INPUTS.json` reports 35 public/protected stimulus files byte-identical to interrupted v0.5 apart from their source-lock reference. Thus the final outcome rests on a fresh complete main pass while retaining earlier failures as separate attempts. These are archived execution receipts, not a local scientific rerun by Codex.

## Limits and accounting

The 6,000-call ceiling was shared across **all** versions: v0.3 45; v0.4 90 fixture plus 1,085 interrupted main; v0.5 the same; v0.6 3,600 complete main/replay = **5,995 attempts**. Two interrupted calls had no acknowledged completion; `audit/ALL_ATTEMPTS.json` charges them, yielding 5,993 acknowledgments and no full retry budget. The tighter v0.6 bound followed the frozen route schedule: eight families × (24 original-query + 21 canonical-query occasions) × five readers = 1,800 forwards per main pass. It narrowed accounting, without dropping condition cells or lifting the cap (`GLOBAL_FORWARD_LEDGER.json`, `protocol/TRANSPORT_RECOVERY_v0.6.md`).

The original shared clock, 2-hour wall limit, 3 GiB process-tree RSS, 3.5 GiB container abort and 256 MiB new-output ceiling were retained. The report gives 1,726.40 seconds through main replay/comparison and 2,145.57 seconds through relocated delivery QA. The largest sampled supervised process-tree RSS was 597,893,120 bytes; interrupted stages lack final supervisor receipts and sampling may miss brief peaks (`audit/RESOURCE_ROLLUP.json`). Relocation QA re-scored main and carried fixture with no model forwards and 32 byte-identical result files, then the final archive identity is handled by a separate delivery receipt (`audit/DELIVERY_QA_PREPACK.json`, `DELIVERY_RELOCATION_RESCORE.json`).

**Interpretation boundary:** W and I have identical held-out answer counts (391/600) and routes; neither establishes unique cognitive value or overall efficiency for a 112-byte packet. X_latest demonstrates a policy distinction about current content, while no-cue wrong intent remains unsolved despite valid public bindings. Cost wins are small Python object-graph observations with opposing parse, allocation and traffic costs. Five model seeds repeat 120 held-out query occasions across 20 sequences, so they are not 600 independent episodes. No native recurrent state, learned writing, compression, original Q010 training requalification or integrated NSS role behavior follows from this result. The frozen Primary/Secondary/Tertiary/Validator roles remain separate.
