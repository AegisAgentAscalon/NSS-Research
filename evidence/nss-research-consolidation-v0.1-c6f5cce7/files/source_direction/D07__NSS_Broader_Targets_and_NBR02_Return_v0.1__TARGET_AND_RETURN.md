## 1. Broader target: a stable foundation that can be renewed deliberately

The intended three-core arrangement remains:

| Component | Intended responsibility and constraint |
|---|---|
| Primary | General competence and identity-bearing reference behavior. Participates in the active phases and remains frozen within each accepted generation. |
| Secondary | Online/adaptive learning and personalized competence; the location and interfaces of recurrent/persistent task state remain design questions rather than a new blanket implementation choice. |
| Tertiary | Dreaming: grounded reflection, practice preparation, consolidation and repair planning, with prospective self-play curriculum generation retained as a separate deferred contribution. |
| Validator and evaluation | The neural Validator supplies fallible advice and evidence challenges. Evaluation measures behavior and costs. Governed qualification, not the neural advisor's assertion, owns adoption. |

Record four distinct timescales within this arrangement [S1, sections 1, 5 and 8]:

1. Ordinary online learning with light, bounded stabilization.
2. Periodic dream consolidation of Secondary and its supporting experience store.
3. Less frequent successor-Primary consolidation of durable acquired competence.
4. Optional growth or replacement of an individual core when its additional capacity is justified.

These are not four new cores, nor four automatically scheduled jobs. The micro–macro toy experiments do not implement these complete lifecycle mechanisms.

## 2. Successor-Primary target

Start a same-shape renewal from a clone of the **latest accepted Primary**, not from the original baseline on every renewal. Use a versioned Secondary teacher and its lawful experience to transfer acquired competence that the current Primary lacks. Balance that material with relevant Primary/general-capability rehearsal and direct current evidence [S1, section 2].

Tertiary organizes and challenges the curriculum. The evaluator measures acquired competence, retained foundations, current corrections, evidence dependence and costs. Validator feedback is advisory. Candidate acceptance requires separate qualification; agreement among teachers/reviewers is not a substitute for source checks or behavioral evaluation.

Teacher dependencies must be declared. A Secondary skill may rely on Primary, retrieval, tools or task state; demonstrations produced by that combined configuration must not be attributed to the bare Secondary checkpoint. Preserve legitimate scoped corrections rather than restoring obsolete Primary defaults. Do not imitate every Secondary output unquestioningly or reset Secondary after a partial transfer.

This creates two complementary, unvalidated teaching paths:

- **Routine dream:** Primary plus current evidence helps repair and advance a Secondary candidate.
- **Generation renewal:** evidence-qualified Secondary experience helps advance a cloned Primary candidate, while retaining the useful foundation.

Neither path implies tensor averaging, exact historical recovery from weights, or a promise of near-complete repair at a particular cost.

## 3. Bounded memory and source-window retirement

Updating Primary and retiring stored experience are **separate decisions** [S1, section 3]. The target is to retire eligible bulk recent-experience material after demonstrating sufficient consolidation, not to assume that a trained checkpoint is a lossless backup of the internal RAG.

A future procedure should preserve the source partition during qualification, define the exact/compact material that will remain, and deny the candidate access to the proposed expired partition during isolated evaluation. Test closed-book behavior only where consolidation into parameters is claimed. Test retrieval-assisted skills with their declared remaining evidence. Include current facts, rare exceptions and acquired methods, not just common examples.

Retain required exact records and unresolved material. Any expiry requires an explicit retention policy and authorization. Storage pressure can initiate preparation; it cannot turn failed qualification into permission to erase the source. No current research archive is deleted, merged, compressed differently or replaced by this addendum.

## 4. Independently expandable cores

Each core may eventually have a larger or different successor [S1, section 6]. Keep enlargement separate from knowledge consolidation. The recorded alternatives are same-size continuation, a specifically supported widening/deepening construction, or training/distillation into a larger or different candidate. No general resizing procedure is selected.

A need affecting one core does not automatically require enlarging all three. Compare additional capacity with credible same-size training improvements and charge the continuing memory, inference, training and maintenance cost. After a Primary revision, establish Secondary/Tertiary/Validator compatibility even when those components do not grow.

Version the tokenizer/codec, inter-core messages, representation interfaces, tool behavior, recurrent state and pending learning traces. Do not assume that equal dimensions establish semantic state compatibility. Preserve qualified fallback and rollback and account for experience arriving during candidate preparation.

The existing Secondary/Tertiary mutual-exclusion rule is retained. Capture teacher behavior, prepare a curriculum, train candidates and evaluate results in explicitly scheduled phases. Training is real work even when the candidate is not serving.

Peak storage must include the accepted cores, candidate, teacher snapshots, optimizer/training state, source window awaiting retirement, tests, logs and rollback. Do not promise that consolidation or larger weights eliminate the disk budget.

## 6. Current research checkpoint: MICROMACRO01, not another generation experiment

The latest source report is MICROMACRO01 scientific protocol v0.1 / execution v0.2 [S2]. The following is a source-report summary, **not a fresh numerical audit**:

| Branch | G qualifying cycles | L qualifying cycles |
|---|---:|---:|
| Original weaker correction B1 | 13/32 | 14/32 |
| Strong correction, no repair U | 25/32 | 17/32 |
| Distributed old-record repair D | 32/32 | 29/32 |
| Same-work late old-record cleanup B4 | 32/32 | 29/32 |
| Distributed extra focal practice F4 | 25/32 | 16/32 |

D and B4 pass the primary bounded full-cycle requirements separately for both methods. The denominators are eight held-out association cases repeated across four fitted models per method, not 32 independent task families. L's three failed cycles remain failures.

The report supports these distinctions:

- D improves the worst recorded mean old-answer loss relative to B4 in 31/32 G and 32/32 L contexts. B4 generally finishes with slightly lower old-answer loss. Neither schedule dominates all measures.
- D and B4 preserve the corrected focal answer and reach the specified recovery range through two further ordinary learning receipts without additional repair.
- U also preserves old class answers in this sample. Much of the repair benefit concerns probability quality, not restoration of previously lost labels.
- Maintenance uses four applications and 32 replay-example gradient calculations per context. Scaling a step down does not reduce the differentiation already performed.
- The primary operating allowance is a study-local choice. A one-point mean recovery sensitivity does not qualify D/B4; a five-point sensitivity also qualifies the cheaper G-U control.
- Conditions restart from initial-fit checkpoints. The result does not establish cumulative lifelong retention, recovered plasticity, unbuffered-skill protection, a real dream phase, or a complete serving system.

The source report records its own engineering history and numerical replay. This documentation step does not reproduce them. Its actual-host date differs from some older conversation-dated notes; the original dates and bytes are preserved rather than silently rewritten.

## 7. The immediate question we return to

The user's objective remains `DEC-NSS-PROGRESS-002` [S3]:

> Find what works, then identify what causes the least degradation, requires the least repair, and yields the most retained improvement.

MICROMACRO01 supplies a bounded feasibility reference. The open problem is **the amount and allocation of repair worth paying for**, not yet cloning Primary, retiring RAG material, or enlarging the cores.

**Reviewer recommendation for the next preparation step, not a selected experiment:** specify a narrow repair-budget comparison asking whether less maintenance can meet the same declared old/new operating requirements. Preserve an unchanged working reference and the cheaper no-repair control; hold the learner, correction policy, lawful records, evaluator and target criteria fixed; vary one repair-budget dimension. Count replay-example gradients, actual applications and whole-stage resource costs separately. Do not call a smaller multiplier a cheaper update unless computation actually decreases.

Choose the particular reduced-work mechanism and freeze its protocol before execution. Retain immediate disruption, stage recovery, worst-record effects, final performance and learning after cleanup. A cheaper candidate is useful only if the retained improvement and relevant losses justify it. A null result may establish that the tested reduction was insufficient; it is not permission for an unbounded search.

No specific count, threshold, seed, new model, or additional fitting budget is selected by this note. This is a return to the research question, not authorization to run another comparison.
