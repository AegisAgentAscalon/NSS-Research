# Contribution 00 — executed change-and-review contract
## C00-CONTRACT01 / execution v0.1 · 25 September 2026

**Result: the bounded synthetic contract-conformance screen passed.** An ordinary deterministic review rule, change record, durable journal and simulated adoption interface were implemented and executed. This is the foundational engineering step requested after the contribution roadmap, not a learned reviewer, a new learning rule, or a completed deployment.

**Zero neural models were loaded. Zero model forwards, new gradients or optimizer updates occurred.** Synthetic candidate behavior tables were deliberately authored, rather than generated by a learner. No historical study was rerun, requalified or modified.

## 1. The scope we could honestly run

The source `program/STEP_00_CONTRACT.md` specifies the old/new change question and record fields, but explicitly leaves operational tolerances and latency limits open. Instead of silently choosing production safety standards, this execution uses caller-supplied numerical limits as engineering test inputs. The study checks whether the software follows those supplied limits. It does not establish which limits NSS should adopt.

The new executable convention is documented in `protocol/PROTOCOL.md`: three old capabilities, one legitimately corrected focal target, binary probability tables in integer thousandths, a rolling parent and a stable epoch anchor. The default illustration allows a 0.05 per-record decrease from the parent and 0.10 from the anchor, with old/focal probability floors of 0.60/0.85. Grid fixtures vary limits. These numbers are synthetic boundary-test values, **not calibrated risk levels, policy adoption, or neural predictions**.

The actual classification is derived from the current public target, not the superseded focal answer. Invalid authority is not exchanged for improved accuracy. No protected record can be sacrificed merely because the average of other records improves.

## 2. What executed

The source/configuration lock preceded fixture construction and execution. The primary pass processed:

* 1,440 deterministic factorial fixture cases, covering tolerances, focal quality, old-quality changes, stable/rolling-reference differences, label orientation and known versus unknown completion.
* 44 literal boundary, provenance, identity and malformed-record cases.
* 14 lifecycle scenarios, including simulated adoption, review-to-adoption staleness, cumulative drift, deadlines, incomplete accounting and two actual subprocess terminations.

These cases are exposed engineering fixtures, not random samples, held-out examples, or independent real tasks. The principal result is reproducible agreement with the written finite contract.

| Review disposition | Cases | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| ADOPT_ELIGIBLE | 256 | The supplied finite-panel requirements are met; separate commit still required. |
| DEFER: behavioral conditions unmet | 479 | The focal requirement or at least one protected per-record condition is unmet. |
| DEFER: incomplete/stale/unresolved evidence | 733 | The rule cannot establish eligibility from a valid current evidence state. |
| REJECT: integrity/schema violation | 16 | The supplied proposal fails the fixture's integrity or schema contract. |
| **Total** | **1,484** | Every decision, reason set, input hash and computed metric matched the independent reference. |

The 733 unresolved cases are **not** 733 proven harmful adaptations. The 479 behavioral cases are author-constructed contract violations, not discovered failures of a trained model.

The independent evaluator is a separate implementation using declarative requirement tables. It imports no production decision, journal or fixture-builder code. Both implementations follow the same written specification, so agreement cannot discover every shared specification error. All 25 additional independent lifecycle assertions passed.

## 3. Bounded leniency was implemented—not perfection of probabilities

One explicit fixture retains all classes while lowering one old target probability from 0.95 to 0.91. Under its supplied 0.05 parent / 0.10 epoch allowances, the candidate is eligible. A correct focal target need not preserve the previous wrong focal answer.

Conversely, a fixture with a larger single protected-record regression is deferred even if other old probabilities improve enough to make an aggregate look favorable. Focal failure is also a deferral: preserving old behavior while never acquiring the new answer is not eligibility.

These are tests of policy semantics, not evidence that those particular changes would be acceptable in an application.

## 4. Cumulative drift cannot be erased by accepting each small step

A chronological fixture starts all protected target probabilities at 0.95. Successive candidates propose 0.91, 0.87 and 0.83. Every individual change is 0.04, within the 0.05 parent allowance.

The first two candidates are eligible and their simulated revisions commit. The third exceeds the unchanged epoch allowance: 0.95 to 0.83 is a 0.12 decline. It is deferred, and the simulated accepted revision remains the second revision. The epoch anchor stays 0.95 throughout.

This illustrates why both references are represented. It does not validate a learned cumulative-risk estimator or prove that probability differences are additive physical harm.

## 5. A favorable review is not an adoption receipt

The normal lifecycle creates one intent, persists the new simulated state, and appends one acknowledgment. Repeating the same operation ID is rejected; no duplicate adoption occurs.

Separate scenarios change the accepted parent, the stable epoch, or the target receipt after review but before adoption. Re-evaluation blocks all three stale review tokens. Eligibility is bound to the current evidence/parent version rather than an unversioned `verified` label.

A deferred bad candidate is retained in the journal while a separately authored, acceptable candidate is later accepted. **That is an evidence-revision scenario, not a model repair**: no training produced the second candidate.

No live serving API or concurrent readers were implemented. The tested `Store` is an ordinary simulated state machine; a real serving publication protocol remains a separate dependency.

## 6. Failure-first checks included actual interrupted processes

Two small child processes intentionally terminate:

1. After the durable adoption intent, before storing the new state.
2. After storing the new state, before recording its completion acknowledgment.

Both are classified `UNKNOWN_COMPLETION`. A retry is blocked rather than automatically repeated. The first retains the old simulated state; the second physically contains the new simulated state but lacks the final acknowledgment. This difference is retained.

**Important limitation:** this does not prove atomic user-visible publication. The second state demonstrates why a deployed serving reader must obey an explicit committed-version protocol rather than blindly load the most recently written file. The pilot has no serving reader, no neural parameter mutation, and no automatic recovery policy that guesses which outcome occurred.

Six copied log corruptions—middle deletion, duplication, reordering, torn final record, suffix truncation and altered payload—were detected when checked against the retained terminal count/hash. A clean log passed. A separately corrupted durable state failed its recorded-state-hash check.

A correctly chained prefix **still passes local hash-chain checks if the external terminal identity is omitted**. This is an explicitly tested limitation, not a failed detector hidden from the result. Hashes supply integrity relative to trusted references; they are not authentication of arbitrary source claims and do not prevent a party rewriting the entire chain and its anchor.

The file operations exercise process interruption, not power loss, hardware failure, all operating systems, hostile multiwriter concurrency or full disk behavior. Those must not be implied by this fixture suite.

## 7. Review coverage is different from solving everything

The synthetic since-last-dream interval initially contains accounted entries 1, 2 and 4, with entry 3 missing. The cursor advances only through 2. Entry 2 remains unresolved and marked shallowly reviewed.

Once entry 3 is explicitly accounted for as deferred, the contiguous cursor can advance through 4. Entries 2 and 3 remain unresolved. Their unresolved status does not vanish with cursor advancement.

No Tertiary core performed these reviews. The test establishes ordinary bookkeeping for its potential future input and continuation cursor. It does not measure triage quality, deep review or autonomous repair.

## 8. The strong controls and the deliberate blind spot

On the same exposed fixture set:

| Fixed decision policy | Eligible candidates blocked | Behavioral violations allowed | Integrity-invalid allowed | Unresolved allowed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Contract rule | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Accept all | 0 | 479 | 16 | 733 |
| Never adopt | 256 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

These are reference-policy counts from the finite cases, not a claim that an intelligent review system achieved perfect real-world discrimination. The rule has the evidence needed to evaluate its own declared contract. There is no competing learned review mechanism yet.

A separately retained pair has **byte-identical public evidence**. One evaluator-only description adds an unaffected unobserved capability; the other says that capability is broken. The operational rule returns the same eligible decision for both. It is wrong on one of the two broader-scope descriptions, because the public panel cannot reveal the distinction.

That probe is a deliberately constructed information limit—not a random 50% performance estimate. If the protected scope is only the disclosed panel, both are eligible within that scope. If wider skill preservation is claimed, the panel is insufficient. The hidden description never enters the reviewer.

## 9. Replay, work and verification

Fresh processes reconstructed the same deterministic fixture files and reran the full ordinary rule/lifecycle/fault prefix. Every deterministic retained raw file, input file and independent result file matched: **46 file comparisons, 12,099,558 bytes per side**. Per-process timing and RSS are excluded from byte equality. This is internal independent implementation checking plus same-runtime replay, not third-party replication.

There were **3,024 actual rule evaluations**, including lifecycle rechecks and child processes, across primary plus replay. There were 64 lifecycle journal events across those passes, plus 5,936 primary-grid review-intent/commit records. All are software operations, **not optimizer steps**. The two deliberately interrupted adoption operations per pass remain unknown and are not falsely counted as committed.

All six supervised generation/run/scoring stages finished without an unexpected stop. Their summed wall time is recorded in `audit/EXECUTION.json`, with sampled process-tree memory every 0.025 seconds. Brief peaks may be missed. Earlier design/code preparation was not continuously timed; the 30-minute cap begins at the source lock. No global efficiency inference follows from such a small instrumented program.

Relocated final-package byte checks and independent retained-data rescoring are documented in the separate delivery receipt. The final report is not itself proof of those later QA checks. Re-scoring uses existing fixture values only and performs no model inference or updates.

## 10. Decision and next boundary

Contribution00 now has an **implemented ordinary engineering baseline** for the concrete public change/decision record, finite-panel rule, cumulative anchor, audit journal and simulated adoption recheck. It is no longer only a prose concept.

The next contribution is still **not automatically authorized**. Before a substantive two-sided reviewer comparison, choose operational harm/availability standards, trustworthy measurement acquisition, guard versus independent confirmation data, and one reviewer intervention under equal evidence. A simple rule remains the strong control; it need not be replaced by a neural mechanism.

Open dependencies identified by this pilot:

* `RET-C00-POLICY-001`: calibrate protected capabilities, behavioral allowance, adoption and repair deadlines for the intended task. Synthetic thresholds do not supply them.
* `RET-C00-COVERAGE-001`: define and evaluate coverage beyond the review panel; repeated operational checks are not untouched confirmation.
* `RET-C00-PUBLISH-001`: implement a real reader/publication/recovery boundary before claiming that interrupted candidates never affect serving.
* `RET-C00-AUTHORITY-001`: acquire and authenticate real evidence rather than relying on fixture authority flags and author-specified measurements.

NBR-01's bounded closeout is unchanged. NBR-02 remains active; all other experiments stay paused. `RET-MW01-FIT-001`, deferred dream/self-play ideas, `OBS-DC01-COST-001`, and the post-NBR-03 foundation return remain intact. Parent architecture and historical data were not modified.

**Bottom line:** we have checked that the first ordinary review scaffold can apply a defined bounded-disruption contract and account for incomplete changes. We have not yet shown that its inputs are sufficient for real capability protection or that a learned reviewer, maintained tokens, rehearsal or Tertiary improves it.
