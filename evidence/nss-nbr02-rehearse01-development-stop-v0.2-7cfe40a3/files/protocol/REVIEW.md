## What deserves caution
Recovery is not prevention. A candidate can meet the proposed post-extra gate while briefly failing between
the strong update and rehearsal. Both states must remain visible and time-to-answer/extra work counted.
A production policy that hides intermediate state from users would be a separate, costed system policy.

The rehearsal batch covers the entire toy old memory. Passing would not show protection of unbuffered
skills or general longevity. Its selection is fixed by software; this is not a learned Tertiary choosing dreams.
Old labels stay stable here. The validity checker has harder synthetic cases but ordinary stream results
cannot establish handling of every real-world stale/conflicting memory.

One small batch may be too weak to restore old confidence or may undo the new answer. That is the
question, not a reason to mix the focal item into R, increase the rehearsal count, change clipping, or
relabel later recovery after looking at results.

The same-record F mean does not receive eight times the update magnitude. Equal extra examples and
applications still do not guarantee equal latency/native memory. R requires distinct record fetches and
possibly different activations; report the actual costs. OBS-DC01-COST-001 remains open.

The normalized A0 removes a mathematical fixed-count ceiling but does not guarantee admission. H may
be0 and small H is statistically coarse. The0.5 threshold is a pragmatic proposal, not a law of plasticity.
No new gate is retroactively applied to the exposed PROTECT01 rows in this review.

R can pass its full recovery screen while F does equally well. That would not establish a special old-record
selection benefit. Report every R−F contrast even when the overall candidate fails; no hidden selection
or hindsight steering/rehearsal superiority threshold is introduced.
