# NBR02-REV01 v0.4: result and interpretation

**Decision:** The one-time larger correction passed the declared first-correction efficacy gate for G and L, but **both failed the complete correction-with-retention screen**. NBR02 remains active. The supplied full execution archive is available in `Research/NSS_NBR02_REV01_Complete_v0.4.zip`; its bytes, physical members, alias descriptors and overlapping score tables were checked separately in this review's machine evidence. The 101 logical alias/encoded targets were not all freshly reconstructed, and no raw numerical replay was run here. This note interprets the returned result, not an independently rerun experiment.

| Exact-gradient G | 17/32 → 32/32 | 256/256 in both branches | Fail: old loss +0.012933 versus allowed +0.007562; margin drop 0.025260 versus 0.014779 | **Fail** |
| Diagonal-local L | 16/32 → 32/32 | clip 1: 256/256; clip 2: 251/256 | Fail: old loss +0.074712 versus allowed +0.010000; margin drop 0.126025 versus 0.020000 | **Fail** |

Each 32 comprises eight base conditions repeated across four fitted seeds, not 32 independent association problems. All four G fits gained (5, 3, 2, 5) and all four L fits gained (5, 6, 2, 3); G improved on eight bases, L on seven. No previously correct first correction reverted. The intervention is well isolated for this toy: paired branches share the same cue-time state, public receipt and raw gradient; all first-change raw norms exceeded 2, so raising the one-time clip from 1 to 2 actually changed step size. The changed label was unknowable before the lawful feedback (0/32 correct in each method), and both branches reached 32/32 after the next same-label receipt. The benefit is **one-receipt faster class correction** in this short schedule, not higher final focal class accuracy or proof that clipping uniquely caused the old DC01 failures.

The retention failure is not cosmetic. G kept all measured old classes but crossed predeclared development-calibrated confidence limits. L's clip-2 branch made five distinct previously correct model/base/cue combinations wrong immediately; those same five appeared at each of four tail checkpoints, producing 20 repeated wrong observations, not 20 independent lost memories. Clip 1 was wrong on one of those combinations at three later checkpoints, leaving 17 additional wrong observations for clip 2. None of the five affected the repeatedly reinforced protected cue, a useful lead but not a causal demonstration that rehearsal would repair them. The complete guard appropriately rejects both methods. The guardrails are pragmatic exploratory tolerances, not confidence intervals or validated deployment limits. G and L arrived from separately learned checkpoints, so their native difference does not isolate the local approximation; an exact update at L's prestate was audited as a gradient but not applied and scored.

The tested model was a 674-parameter float32 tanh network on START/PRIME/CUE, with four fit seeds per method (eight G/L fits), four development and eight held-out bases. A public last-observed-label table triggered the correction; no hidden reversal marker reached the learner. All old targets in an ensuing protection proposal must come from earlier released receipts. The causal exact table X can answer after those receipts, but cannot know an unreleased association or unannounced reversal. Fresh maps and seeds do not imply a new abstract task: all six-label condition patterns overlap DC01's small binary grammar. Repeated inventory queries, fixture delays, and replay are not additional independent held-out samples.

Execution history matters. v0.3 stopped **before main fits** when its conservative delivery forecast exceeded 64 MiB. v0.4 changed the lossless evidence codec, not model, seeds, labels, threshold or scientific comparison; it restarted the scientific work fresh, retained the stopped attempt, and charged both attempts plus adaptive replay. The all-attempt ledger reports 4,024 committed updates under 4,500, with no unknown update completion. The report's replay and independent scalar audit support reproducibility within its declared numerical tolerance, not a second independent sample. The complete archive is supplied here, unlike the earlier compact-only DC01 handoff. Its numerical evidence does not establish overall speed or memory advantage: the inherited Python ownership counter still misses some native graph/backing allocations (`OBS-DC01-COST-001`).

The supplied post-result candidate packet reasonably targets selective correction, but its referenced `PROTECT01_TECHNICAL.md` was absent from the compact handoff, the full study ZIP inventory and the two standalone files provided to this review. Its equations and numerical contract therefore remain proposal-review items, not accepted source. The Tertiary dream/maintenance concept is documented as `RET-DREAM-MAINT-001` **DEFERRED_DESIGN**; REV01 ran no Tertiary, rehearsal selection, maintenance fit or architecture integration. NBR01 stays closed only at bounded recovered-evidence scope, `RET-MW01-FIT-001` remains parked, and frozen Primary/Secondary/Tertiary/advisory Validator boundaries remain intact.
