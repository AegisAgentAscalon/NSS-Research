# NBR-01 — MembershipRepair008
## Familiarity-aware repair and writable novelty · v0.1
**Executed 21 September 2026 · Main comparison RUN · Full joint target NOT MET · NBR-01 remains OPEN**

## 1. Decision brief
Selective repair worked for ordinary independent unregistered inputs while preserving nominal storage recovery, useful eight-round relationship traversal, and the ordinary registration/write/clear lifecycle. But it rejected too many familiar queries at the declared q20 disturbance. No primary or crossed candidate passed the complete joint criteria in any of its five paired runs.

A second boundary is important: a near-duplicate identity can be properly registered yet a fuzzy learned binder can still write its relationship into an older record. A plausible correct final answer can hide that structural error. The post-hoc diagnostic in §7 isolates it without modifying the main scores.

**Keep:** selective admission as a bounded precision-oriented option, explicit active-reference/absence/readability semantics, the compatible repair/readers, and the strong nearest-code control. **Revise:** heavy-query handling and near-identity binding. Do not promote either head as a general solution to familiarity, and do not declare NBR-01 complete.

This study is entirely numerical. “Identity” means a generated code in this experiment, not a person's identity. “Unknown” means no supported record/match under the declared inputs, not proof that something does not exist or is false. Numerical noise is a synthetic stress condition, not a biological or hardware fault-rate model.

## 2. What actually ran
Five new 633-parameter match heads, seeds 1001–1005, were each fitted for 1,200 AdamW steps (batch 256): **6,000 main optimizer steps and 1,536,000 training presentations**. Five simple geometric gates were independently calibrated on the same held-out calibration evidence used by their corresponding learned gates. Each calibration had 4,096 examples in each of seven classes: **143,360 main calibration labels in total**. Learned gates additionally consumed their counted training data; this is not an equal-teaching-cost contest.

The original Study006 readers and binders 801–805 and Study007 learned restorers 901–905 stayed frozen. Every learned parameter stayed fixed during scored evaluation. Candidate heads use nearest distance, competing-match separation, cosine features, norm, the existing UNKNOWN coordinate and active-reference count; no true origin, noise setting, original clean vector, target slot, hidden graph or correct answer enters the resolver.

The primary comparison changes admission, not the neural reader. To isolate admission, headline tables pair each gate with nearest-code projection. Gate/restorer cross combinations were also run with the old learned dictionary restorer; none changed the joint decision. Stage A retains distance-only and margin-only ablations without new tuning. No robust-reader retraining was performed.

The original research plan is `spec/RESEARCH_PLAN_v0.1.md`; the resolved protocol is `spec/EXECUTION_PROTOCOL.md`. The local `EXECUTION_LOCK.json` records source/checkpoint/calibration hashes and was created before the main episodes were scored. This was not an external preregistration. All main seeds were retained. The pilot exposed the q20 tradeoff; the 95% core requirement was deliberately retained, not reduced after seeing failure.

One completed development fit (1,200 steps) and a later exact training reproduction (another 1,200) are separate from the main fit count. The first development pipeline found an implementation error in the UNKNOWN competitor; the pre-main fix restored the inherited sentinel semantics. Failed pilot logs remain in the evidence folder. One foundation-suite invocation was interrupted by the tool command timeout and was rerun completely; no main model fit was excluded or replaced.

Each entry below is the mean across five pairs, each using 1,024 fresh independent episodes for that stratum. Exact known, mildly altered known, and independent unregistered vectors share the same underlying unit-vector code convention. Factual graph relationships are not supplied to the resolver.

| Mechanism | Exact known | Mild known q05 | Altered known q20 | False acceptance: independent unregistered |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unconditional nearest | 100.00% | 100.00% | 98.85% | 100.00% |
| Unconditional learned repair | 100.00% | 100.00% | 98.85% | 100.00% |
| A: geometric gate + nearest | 100.00% | 100.00% | 70.16% | 0.43% |
| B: learned gate + nearest | 100.00% | 100.00% | 66.93% | 0.55% |

The geometric gate accepted **22/5,120** independent unregistered inputs; the learned gate accepted **28/5,120**. The same-base noisy-independent negative stratum gave A 0.41% and B 0.43%. All five pairs met the *empirical*1% identity false-acceptance ceiling on both ordinary negative strata. Those observations do not prove a deployment error probability below 1%.

The q20 supported identity losses are decisive: A 70.16% and B 66.93%, versus 98.85% for unconditional projection. Abstention counts as failure on a supported identity. B did not earn a nominal advantage over A in this preparation. These are bounded implementations, not proof that a better learner or richer input cannot improve the tradeoff.

The larger Stage A membership sample and smaller end-to-end query sample are different cohorts. On the end-to-end q20 case, record_hop's supported destination accuracy was A 75.63% and B 71.25%. No condition was relabelled stress after these failures.

Raw pre-projection scores, accepted identities and final selected codes are saved. A canonical repaired vector is not used as independent evidence that the original input was familiar. Unconditional controls naturally propose a known symbol for every independent unit-vector input; that is different from a final reader making a room claim.

## 4. The useful memory/reading result was preserved
These headline state conditions use 64 streams/pair (32 complete and 32 missing) and the inherited record_hop reader at sufficient depth. Parent and gate weights are fixed. Both fields receive sigma 0.02 disturbance per tick in the nominal storage rows.

| Mechanism | 64 ticks, known/missing | 1,024 ticks + eight links, known/missing | Room claims for unregistered query |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unconditional nearest | 100.00% / 100.00% | 100.00% / 100.00% | 67.50% |
| Unconditional learned repair | 100.00% / 100.00% | 100.00% / 100.00% | 49.38% |
| A: geometric gate + nearest | 100.00% / 100.00% | 100.00% / 100.00% | 0.00% |
| B: learned gate + nearest | 100.00% / 100.00% | 100.00% / 100.00% | 0.00% |

Both gates preserved 100% tested known and missing-chain outcomes through 1,024 nominal noise ticks and eight-link reasoning. The unregistered-query row contains 320 queries in total: zero unsupported room outputs were observed with either selective gate, compared with 67.50% for unconditional nearest and 49.38% for unconditional learned repair. Zero/320 is not zero risk; the descriptive 95% Wilson upper endpoint is about 1.19%.

The gated_hop reader and exact traversal of the **same actual store** were also evaluated. Exact traversal received no gold rewrite of the stored associations. The two neural readers still need sufficient query depth: repairing symbols does not itself traverse the graph. All 1/2/4/8 outputs remain available in SUMMARY.json and per-pair results.

The registered whole-joint screens remain **0/5 for each of the four selective gate/restorer configurations**. They pass ordinary missing-information and registration requirements, but fail q20 supported coverage and the allowed known-repair regression. This is a partial mechanism success, not a passed overall experiment.

## 5. Writable novelty and evidence status
The active reference initially contains 12 identities and four zero, masked rows. The new code is unavailable to the candidate until a distinct permitted REGISTER event; querying it cannot register it. Registration changes scoped working state, not model weights. REGISTER creates no location fact. Qualified relationship writes use the inherited learned binder, without a target slot supplied by the scorer.

| Phase | A task accuracy | B task accuracy |
|---|---|---|
| before_register | 100.00% | 100.00% |
| after_register | 100.00% | 100.00% |
| after_write | 100.00% | 100.00% |
| correction | 100.00% | 100.00% |
| clear | 100.00% | 100.00% |
| rewrite | 100.00% | 100.00% |
| reset | 100.00% | 100.00% |
| fresh | 100.00% | 100.00% |

These ordinary registration scores are 100% in every pair for A-nearest and B-nearest. Neighbour-record checks and the crossed repair arms also meet their registered 95% floor. The lifecycle includes 64 background/noise ticks per phase, denied and wrong-scope writes, corrections, clear/rewrite, full scope reset and fresh setup. Checks occur at declared query points; this is not an all-time guarantee.

Identity membership, relationship presence and readability remain distinct. A rejected stored field retains its presence and available raw vector but becomes sticky-unreadable until a qualified write/clear. Repeated projection of the same rejected state cannot manufacture additional evidence. This conservative policy contributes to the cadence failure below; it is a declared policy, not the only plausible one.

Protected Boolean relation-presence is still an explicit control. The learned existence maps from Study005 were not silently transplanted. The codebook remains a protected reference of symbols, not a protected graph or truth oracle. Old masks/status snapshots are rejected under the new ABI rather than assumed compatible.

## 6. Maintenance can be less frequent—but not arbitrarily delayed
The following uses the same 1,024-tick/eight-link graph family and sigma 0.02 schedule. It is a predeclared schedule comparison, not a search for a best result after failure.

| Schedule | A supported answers | B supported answers | A/B missing answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Every tick | 100.00% | 100.00% | 100.00% / 100.00% |
| Every 8 ticks | 100.00% | 100.00% | 100.00% / 100.00% |
| Every 32 ticks | 40.00% | 10.62% | 100.00% / 100.00% |
| Read time only | 0.00% | 0.00% | 100.00% / 100.00% |

Every-eight-tick repair made 128 maintenance sweeps instead of 1,024 and preserved all tested nominal chain outcomes. That is **eightfold fewer sweeps**, not a measured eightfold whole-system speedup. Evidence processing, queries, matching, allocation and bookkeeping still cost time.

At 32 ticks, accumulated distortion made the selective policies reject fields and quarantine them. Unconditional nearest still achieved 99.38% supported-chain accuracy in that condition, whereas A fell to 40.00% and B to 10.63%. Their good UNKNOWN rates do not offset losing established facts. A familiarity gate suitable for a fresh external query is not automatically a suitable recovery rule for previously established internal state after a longer unobserved interval.

Read-time-only repair after the full delay recovered 3.75% with unconditional nearest and 0% with either gate. The 32-tick contrast is especially diagnostic because much of the state remained recoverable under the unselective control. No post-main cadence or threshold refit replaced these results.

## 7. Two distinct near-identity failures
### Unregistered inputs resembling a known code
At the near-novel0.1 setting, both gates accepted all 5,120 such unfamiliar inputs as registered symbols. End-to-end, both produced unsupported room answers in 50% of the 320 near-novel queries. Half of the base graph queries deliberately lacked an answer, which explains why not every false identity match became a room claim.

The gates work well when the input is far from the registered set; they do not know its historical origin merely because it is close. The identical-observable pairing is a deliberately constructed information-limit illustration. Its 50% binary-origin ceiling is an analytical consequence of opposite labels for identical observations, not an empirically discovered global bound on recognition.

B did improve particular stress tradeoffs: for equal two-code mixtures, its false acceptance was 0.21%, versus 6.41% for A. It preserved 100% identity recognition under doubled vector magnitude, where A rejected all. Both rejected all quarter-magnitude inputs. These useful differences do not rescue B's failure on the core q20 criterion.

### Registered near-duplicates: a correct answer can conceal an overwritten record
Both the unconditional and B-nearest neural readers answered the new near-registered query correctly in 100% of main cases. Yet exact traversal of the same stored records answered only 11.875%. The mismatch triggered a labelled **post-hoc failure-isolation diagnostic**, with no training or changes to the main scores.

In 320 replayed near-registration cases, B-nearest's binder accepted all new writes but used an existing physical record slot in **282 cases (88.125%)**. Only 38 had a separate new record. It actually changed an older record's content in **246 cases (76.875%)**. The fuzzy reader could retrieve the newly written room through that older near-identical key, so the answer looked right despite the incorrect binding.

This is a binder/interface failure, not a restoration win and not evidence that registration is generally solved for near-duplicates. A's stricter margin rejected most such writes; one calibration pair without a margin admitted them and showed the same aliasing risk. The registry itself allocated distinct identity rows correctly; the subsequent relationship binder conflated them. See `evidence/NEAR_COLLISION_DIAGNOSTIC.json` and its independently rerun diagnostic arrays.

| Unconditional nearest | 1.020 | 0.999–1.168 |

These are local implementation measurements on Linux CPU with one Torch compute thread. Timings include validation/Python overhead; other reproduction work was running on the same host. Batch-one and complete-query measurements, percentile ranges and operator allocation profiles are in `evidence/RESOURCES.json`. Do not extrapolate to the user's device or treat these as optimized kernels.

A was lighter than B for a field sweep in this measurement, and the inherited learned projection was considerably more expensive than nearest projection. Query-time differences were smaller/noisier. Candidate complexity must be justified by the quality/error tradeoff, not a neural label.

Raw active state is **3,336 bytes per stream**:2,176 bytes for 16 keys/values,48 for presence/readability flags,1,088 for registered-code capacity,16 for active masks and 8 for the reference revision. Query state, scores/features, projections, model weights, framework and audit storage are additional. The head contributes 2,532 parameter bytes. Float32 head parameters + gradients + two AdamW moments + six scalar steps total 10,152 raw bytes during fitting, excluding inputs/activations/temporaries.

The completed main head fitting loops took **21.01 seconds** in aggregate; calibration and development/reproduction work are additional. The main driver (all membership/state evaluations) took 653.26 seconds. The largest reported main evaluation process peak was **432.3 MiB RSS**, below the registered 4 GiB process ceiling. Driver and concurrently running verification processes are additional; peak RSS is not just neural-state memory. No low-bit learning or packed execution claim follows from these numbers.

## 10. Interpretation and next decision
The proposed gate prevented most ordinary unsupported matches without dismantling the successful clean repair/read/write lifecycle. The hard cases show why it is not a universal solution:

* A damaged known query and a genuinely new near-query can overlap in the available representation. Tightening one scalar cannot create missing origin information.
* Recovery of a previously established internal field and admission of an external query have different histories. A single stationary gate plus permanent quarantine can throw away state that a less restrictive decoder could recover.
* Membership registration and record binding are separate. A correct registry row does not guarantee that a fuzzy binder allocates a distinct relationship record.

**Next candidate direction, not yet executed:** distinguish trusted internal-state recovery from external identity admission under explicit provenance; add independently informative context or redundant identity evidence for ambiguous inputs; and test identity-preserving allocation/selection against a learned binder trained on near-duplicate negatives. Exact identity-aware bookkeeping should remain a labelled control, not be smuggled in as learned reasoning. Evaluate affected old records, not just the new query's answer.

Do not launch an unlimited threshold search, secretly retrain the frozen readers, or replace the tri-core roles. Retain A as a useful low-cost scoped reference and B's limited stress advantages. The full NBR-01 study remains open for robust representation/binding, stronger generic controls, broader tasks and a complete quality/resource comparison. Delayed credit and 1–8-bit learning-state experiments remain deferred.
