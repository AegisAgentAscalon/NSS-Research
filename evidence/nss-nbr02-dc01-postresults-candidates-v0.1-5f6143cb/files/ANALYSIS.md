# DC01 v0.5: bounded adaptation pass and its remaining fault

The supplied compact handoff reports a **completed bounded scientific pass** under the actual integrated v0.3 protocol: A0, D1, D2-G, D2-L and D3 all passed. Four G and four L initial fits passed immediate-feedback admission; the held-out delay-zero post-feedback primary scores were G 302/320 and L 301/320, compared with 224/320 for each corresponding frozen initial-fit control. Both learners retained 512/512 unrelated-old endpoint labels, and the local rule fell within its predeclared noninferiority margins on this small task. These 320 readouts repeat 80 unique occasions across four fits. The all-delay 906/960 and 903/960 totals repeat the same trajectories at three filler lengths; they are not three times as much independent performance evidence.

D1's exact 0/8/32 invariance is valuable implementation evidence, but the protocol made it predictable: one identified receipt, unchanged cue-time parameters, retained cue-time graph/eligibility, detached fillers and state reset after receipt. It does **not** show long-range credit attribution among competing actions, stale-weight learning, or naturalistic retention. G uses an exact gradient through three events before the cue; L implements a diagonal recurrent sensitivity that drops cross-neuron derivative paths. At L's own weights, its mean encoder-gradient discrepancy from exact was 0.7668 in relative norm, yet behavior met D3 here. That is a bounded approximation result, not proof the omitted paths are dispensable on deeper tasks.

The main negative is the first **post-feedback** response after abrupt reversal. Every learned primary miss at delay zero occurred there: G corrected 14/32 and L 13/32, leaving 18 and 19 errors across four fits; after the second corrective receipt, both reached 32/32. The before-feedback response to an unannounced reversal was wrong in all 32/32 cases per method as expected under the information contract, but it is a different score. The report says every failed first corrective update matched its specified gradient, reduced loss and still did not cross the class boundary. All first-reversal gradients were clipped at norm 1. This makes correction dose a useful hypothesis, **not a proven cause**: margin, direction and shared-parameter interference could matter too. The 32 readouts per method arise from paired bases and fits, not 32 independent association families.

Class-label retention alone understates cost: old-cue endpoint accuracy was perfect while mean old-inventory log loss rose from about 0.00236 for frozen controls to 0.01618 for G and 0.03879 for L. Before-feedback pooled accuracy improved over frozen controls, yet mean log loss was worse (G 0.7148, L 0.7247 versus 0.5682/0.5862), dominated by confident surprise at reversal. Any correction-speed proposal must keep raw pre-feedback loss, old-cue loss/margins and post-feedback accuracy separate. A stronger update that flips the focal label but damages stable associations is a tradeoff, not an unconditional win.
