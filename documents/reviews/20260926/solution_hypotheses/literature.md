# Mechanism literature for NSS: competing, testable directions

Prepared 26 September 2026 as bounded documentary analysis. The sources below are primary papers or author/publisher-hosted records. Published benchmark findings motivate candidate mechanisms; they do not establish that the same mechanism transfers to NSS. This document does not dispatch a study or adopt a new architecture. Local result values and status below follow `RESEARCH_INDEX.md` and the accepted `ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW.md`; study archives remain authoritative.

## The engineering problem the evidence actually poses

The latest failure is a continuation problem across changed weights, not simply an inability to fetch the initially saved vector. In STATEBRIDGE01, changed-stage initial reads were 1,024/1,024, but the frozen original whole-prefix numerical check failed 11 array comparisons and stopped the scorer. After eight transitions, CARRY read 610/1,024 states, then 670/1,024 after fixed repair. CARRY performed better than REBUILD on focal A/B bookkeeping, both after learning B (4,050/5,120 vs 2,503/5,120) and after repair (4,519/5,120 vs 3,355/5,120), but post-repair adequacy still failed for both (9/32 and 1/32). PINNED retained the old A result while serving the obsolete B answer. These are diagnostic results, not qualification. A terminal response match, an initial state parse, or a fluent replay is not evidence that a revised transition operator can continue an old task.

Other studies show why a single “protect old knowledge” penalty will not settle the design. MICROMACRO01 passed its prospective three-point old-record recovery contract in 32/32 G and 29/32 L cases, and the new gain survived two receipts, but stricter recovery failed; no-repair retained old classes in that grid. TEACH01's fixed Primary surrogate plus recent-record rehearsal passed its own bounded screen, yet REPLAY and other treatments also passed and no-deep scored 29/32. The teacher's modest omitted-record log-loss benefit therefore does not show that a separately parameterized Tertiary is necessary. PROTECT01 and related repair work also reveal acquisition/retention tradeoffs. Source-backed exact evidence, parameter skill, transient state, update statistics, and future capacity to learn should remain separately measured.

## Competing mechanisms

**1. Freeze the recurrent operator and adapt a readout.** Jaeger's Echo State report describes a recurrent network whose internal dynamics stay fixed while supervised learning modifies output weights using regression; its author-hosted corrected version identifies itself as a corrected copy of 2001 GMD Report 148, with an erratum. This is the cleanest “protect dynamics by construction” extreme. It is a useful comparator because it can separate output-surface changes from recurrent-transition changes, but it may lack the capacity to learn NSS corrections that require changing internal computation. A reservoir is also not a pretrained transformer or a companion memory system.

**Hypothesis:** most of the behavioral continuation damage in STATEBRIDGE is caused by changing recurrent transitions, not by the B readout alone. The saved 2×2 shared/copied readout × adaptive/frozen recurrence diagnostic already recorded in the review directly tests that claim. Report the original implementation/reference numerical failure as historical evidence, separately from behavior. For a future run, declare a prospective numerical contract and held-out behavioral checks before execution; the original failed tolerance need not be reused as the acceptance rule. Score A continuation, B correction, and stale-answer risk; do not select on focal answer counts alone. A frozen recurrence that retains A while failing to learn B is a capacity tradeoff, not a pass.

**2. Adapt small surfaces, then constrain old functions.** LoRA freezes pretrained base weights and adds trainable low-rank matrices at selected Transformer layers; the paper reports fewer trainable parameters and lower optimizer memory for its language-model tasks. That makes adapter-only training a plausible bounded alternative to whole-model updates. However, inserting adapters inside a recurrent block changes its activation and transition function. Freezing the original tensor does not guarantee that saved hidden state has the same interpretation or trajectory. The narrower experiment is to compare readout-only, post-recurrent adapter, and internal adapter arms while explicitly testing state continuation.

Learning without Forgetting (LwF) provides a different mechanism: the old model's outputs on new-task examples act as targets while new capability is learned without retaining old training data. This is functional regularization at the observed outputs rather than parameter importance regularization. It can preserve behavior where old and new inputs overlap, but it cannot constrain old states or input regions absent from that data. For NSS, candidate penalties should include sequence-level old-logit or action-distribution agreement over fixed anchor trajectories, not only terminal class agreement. Compare with an unconstrained same-budget update and exact retrieval. Neither LoRA nor LwF should be called a continuity guarantee.

**3. Keep exact episodes fast; consolidate reusable skill slowly.** iCaRL combines a bounded exemplar set with incremental representation learning and distillation on class-incremental vision benchmarks. RETRO is a stronger topology counterexample: a single autoregressive Transformer consults an external nearest-neighbor corpus through retrieval modules. The two papers show different engineering points on a spectrum: retain a small selected sample for incremental rehearsal, or leave a larger source corpus external and retrieve relevant evidence at use time. NSS's exact owner-keyed evidence and retrieval studies remain the more appropriate factual baseline; its learned systems must beat that baseline on correction/use latency, selection errors, total resident/transient cost, and source provenance before being called more efficient.

**Hypothesis:** keep corrections in an exact, versioned episodic store as soon as they arrive; allow a candidate to consolidate repeated, independently supported patterns into weights during a bounded maintenance window. The store remains the answer source until a candidate passes old and new checks. MICROMACRO01 supplies a local, threshold-sensitive repair precedent; TEACH01 suggests a possible rehearsal component but does not settle whether teacher outputs add value over exact records. Separate the store-on / store-off and practice / no-practice effects. A retrieval-augmented single backbone with role or task modes is a genuine challenger to three separately parameterized cores: it could provide Primary, repair, and review behavior through one model plus explicit retrieval and rules. It could also concentrate error and create role leakage. Compare matched role coverage, evidence access, serving latency, and full memory/compute—not module counts or parameter totals alone. Keep the historic tri-core arrangement fixed in the existing research line; this is a future versioned comparison only.

**4. Distill whole behavior, not just the final answer.** Policy Distillation trained a smaller network to imitate one or more reinforcement-learning teacher policies and reported Atari performance. It supports using a teacher to compress action preferences across encountered states; it does not establish hidden-state identity, a learner's exact event memory, or safe continuation after parameter changes. A prospective state-bridge method could distill the teacher's action/output distributions across complete anchored prefixes, then start from the old carried state. Output/behavior trajectories are the safer first target. Hidden-vector matching is an auxiliary option only if the old and new coordinates are shown to align. Evaluate intermediate behavior, every continuation step, and recovery after correction under a prospectively declared numerical contract; preserve and report STATEBRIDGE's original numerical failure separately. Compare against direct carry, replay/rebuild, and a non-distilled candidate at matched training data and compute.

**5. Quantize accumulated updates last, with an exact reference.** The 4-bit optimizer-state study quantizes Adam-like first and second moments, addressing outlier structure with row/column information and special treatment of second-moment zero points. Its arXiv v3 describes camera-ready revisions for NeurIPS 2023 and reports comparable benchmark accuracy with reduced optimizer memory. That result concerns optimizer moments in its tested training workloads; it does not test NSS eligibility traces, recurrent hidden states, or accumulated correction signals. The earlier NSS low-bit references reach a similar boundary: optimizer-state compression is not trace qualification.

**Hypothesis:** first identify a small adaptive surface that preserves the continuation gate; only then compare high-precision versus low-bit update state on that same surface. Keep the full-precision candidate and an exact cumulative update record as controls. Measure actual persistent bytes, peak scratch, scales/metadata, rounding, zero-crossings, sign reversals, and the cumulative discrepancy after many updates. A two-bit state that matches one update but erases repeated small corrections is a failure. If a high-precision sidecar/error residual is needed, count it in total cost instead of reporting the nominal bitwidth alone.

**6. Preserve the ability to learn, not only old answers.** Dohare et al. demonstrated loss of plasticity across prolonged supervised image-task and reinforcement-learning benchmarks: standard deep-learning systems became less able to fit subsequent tasks, while continual backpropagation and other variability-preserving methods mitigated the effect in the tested settings. The authors distinguish plasticity from stability; their continual-backpropagation method does not itself solve forgetting. This matters because aggressively freezing the recurrent core, adding strict output constraints, or consolidating too rarely could preserve old behavior while preventing the needed B correction. Conversely, unit reinitialization can disrupt stored NSS skills. A future lifecycle evaluation should track both old-anchor retention and learning speed on genuinely new tasks across many updates, rather than treating retention as the only safety metric.

## Best discriminating order and limits

The strongest first discriminator is still the saved STATEBRIDGE 2×2, because it localizes the changed computation while keeping the known numerical and behavioral failures distinct. If frozen recurrence preserves A behavioral continuation while B learns, subject to the new prospectively declared numerical contract passing, test the smallest post-recurrent adaptive surface next. If the readout arm alone cannot learn the correction, compare an internal adapter and an output/trajectory-regularized update. If neither preserves behavioral continuation, test state-anchored continuation rehearsal/distillation only after making its target behavior trajectory and criterion explicit. Preserve pinned-old-revision execution as containment and separately test whether current retrieval prevents stale answers during a delayed update cycle.

The next learning-scale question should compare exact episodic storage plus selective candidate consolidation against always-retrieval and ordinary retraining controls. Add a shared-backbone retrieval/mode arm to test whether the role separation earns its parameter, orchestration, and cross-version costs. After that, test plasticity over repeated maintenance cycles and only then low-bit traces. Every comparison needs versioned sources and model identities, fixed development versus evaluation cases, matched information and compute, all old/new failures, full costs, and predeclared criteria. Chat retains execution authority under the workspace workflow; these are hypotheses, not run instructions. No cited result establishes NSS transfer, proves the tri-core concept, or resolves original Q010 training/checkpoint gaps.
