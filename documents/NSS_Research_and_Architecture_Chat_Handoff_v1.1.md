## Research history and evidence map

### How to read the evidence

A result is attributed to the study report and archive that produced it. A report copied into the consolidation ZIP remains report-level evidence when its full execution archive is missing. Internal replay, independent scoring code, a fresh file-hash check, archive restoration, third-party replication and product qualification are different things. The present consolidation performed no scientific execution. The 2026-09-22 Q001–Q009 audit streamed ZIP members and verified each study’s declared manifest; it did not rerun those studies. Current indexes record that a scoped inventory found some full study archives absent, but the external search had 91 access-denied or vanished metadata errors and exclusions. That is a bounded search result, not proof that no copy exists anywhere.

Values below retain each study’s original denominator and comparator. Repeated seeds, queries, snapshots, arrays, replay passes and duplicated task templates are not automatically independent tasks. Development results do not become held-out confirmation, and a post-result analysis does not become a new fit. The source tags identify exact retained members in the original study archive or in the documentary/source-support archive.

### Starting point: governed architecture, exact records, and open low-bit state

The broad target was named “Tri-Core Validator NSS xTREME” on the MegaMind Alpha track. Its adopted role direction has a frozen, participating Primary; a task and learning Secondary; a predictive and reflective Tertiary that is mutually exclusive with the Secondary; and a separate frozen, fallible advisory Validator. Exact memory is intended to preserve evidence, learned recurrent state to carry evolving work, and a bounded explicit workspace to organize tasks and references. These roles and boundaries describe the desired system. They do not establish that three trained cores, a neural Validator, or an integrated companion were built or compared.

The early research premise deliberately mixed learned mechanisms with exact software references and controls. Registration, ownership, scope, permitted writes, task routing, resets, source provenance, deterministic retrieval and lifecycle policy could be explicit so experiments could isolate what the learned state or reader contributed. The aim was not to call deterministic metadata “neural memory”; it was to keep an answer’s evidence and ownership inspectable while testing learned representation, binding, recurrence, state maintenance and adaptation. The studies gradually separated exact evidence, approximate numerical content, recurrent task state, management workspace and changing parameters.

Low-bit learning state was an important early ambition, not an achieved result. G12 asks whether traces and updates retain useful information at one to two bits. The Q-series, TS-series and NBR-02 reports here use ordinary numerical/floating-point machinery; their storage counts do not qualify quantized traces, sparse execution or low-bit updates. The same restraint applies to biological analogies: G01 asks which mechanisms have support in humans, other mammals or flies, but none of these synthetic experiments tests biological transfer. ### NBR-01: state, memory, binding and repair (Q001–Q009)

The first nine studies formed a progression from tiny persistent state toward an explicit owned-record system. All nine versioned study ZIPs remain present. The 22 September archival audit checked 187, 383, 382, 406, 494, 705, 244, 435 and 501 declared files respectively, with no missing, mismatched or unlisted regular members. That check establishes retained-file integrity, not a fresh scientific replication. Detailed historical outcomes and report member anchors are also preserved in the support archive. [local source: Research/NSS_WorkspaceSupport_v0.1.zip::.research_review/2026-09-22/experiment_history.md]

**Q001 — can a small state preserve a written symbol through distractors, and does learned depth help?** Five-seed synthetic WRITE/DISTRACTOR/QUERY pilots compared GRU, soft-gate, hard-gate and Transformer readers with exact memory. GRU, soft gate and Transformer reached short-task competence in five of five seeds; hard gate from scratch did so in one of five. At 128 distractors, depth-four early-cue recall fell to 0.39% for GRU and 0% for soft gate, while the full-tape Transformer averaged 63.05%. A post-pilot intervention closing low soft gates restored recall on its tested long-delay cases, but exact memory also stayed perfect. This was an initial feasibility signal, not an architecture winner; it motivated explicit event admission and longer addressed streams. [local source: Research/NSS_NBR01_StateDepth_Pilot001_v0.1.zip::NSS_NBR01_StateDepth_Pilot001_v0.1/evidence/SUMMARY.json; Research/NSS_WorkspaceSupport_v0.1.zip::.research_review/2026-09-22/experiment_history.md]

**Q002 — can addressed writable state last through long streams while representing missing and uncertain values?** Five-seed preparations used four explicit addresses, eight values plus UNKNOWN, scored admission and resets, and compared against full-history attention and exact controls through 4,096 distractors. Wider-prepared soft state, calibrated hard gates and exact controls retained clean records at 100%. Short-prepared soft state returned a known value for an empty record in the longest missing-record condition (0% UNKNOWN). A full-history cross-attention reader failed the short competence gate in all five seeds. A fixed hard threshold rejected all weak invalid writes but also all weak valid writes; a threshold calibrated with separate labelled admission examples passed both. A noise-informed belief control reached 62.73% under corrupted scores, with generator/noise information unavailable to the learned readers. The study showed that preparation, qualification and read semantics must be separated; it did not show that hard gates generally win. [local source: Research/NSS_NBR01_HorizonBinding002_v0.1.zip::NSS_NBR01_HorizonBinding002_v0.1/evidence/results/SUMMARY.json; Research/NSS_WorkspaceSupport_v0.1.zip::.research_review/2026-09-22/experiment_history.md]

**Q003 — can a learned maintenance map repair noisy known codes without losing UNKNOWN?** Numerical nine-code states were damaged by Gaussian noise and compared with nearest-prototype and redundancy controls. After one sigma-1 shock and four repair steps, learned recurrence lifted known accuracy from 65.01% to 94.64%, while UNKNOWN accuracy fell from 71.33% to 47.43%; nearest-prototype decoding reached 96.29% known and 77.35% UNKNOWN. After 1,024 sigma-0.15 disturbances, recurrence reached 100% known but 0% UNKNOWN, while nearest-code maintenance reached 100% on both. A later protected-presence intervention restored both labels by adding exact metadata. Fast/slow state did not consistently beat equal-byte averaging. The result motivated a writable empty-state lifecycle and explicit presence/readability semantics, rather than treating denoising as sufficient. [local source: Research/NSS_NBR01_StateRecovery003_v0.1.zip::NSS_NBR01_StateRecovery003_v0.1/SUMMARY.json; Research/NSS_WorkspaceSupport_v0.1.zip::.research_review/2026-09-22/experiment_history.md]

**Q004 — can a writable record remain absent under noise and still support first write, correction, clear and rewrite?** Five paired seeds tested a complete four-address lifecycle, including a 4,096-round empty interval. Original content recurrence plus dynamically protected presence and nearest-prototype maintenance passed the registered joint screens in five of five seeds. Protected presence was 100% known/UNKNOWN at phase endpoints and 99.69% every-read correctness (319/320 streams); nearest-prototype maintenance was 100% throughout. Revised neural empty-contraction arms passed the nominal joint screen in only one of five seeds, and localized empty-gain arms failed the clean long-maintenance baseline in three of five. Local state stability did not guarantee lifecycle correctness, so the next study froze content and isolated the existence lane. [local source: Research/NSS_NBR01_WritableEmpty004_v0.1.zip::NSS_NBR01_WritableEmpty004_v0.1/SUMMARY.json; Research/NSS_WorkspaceSupport_v0.1.zip::.research_review/2026-09-22/experiment_history.md]

**Q005 — can existence be learned separately while content remains frozen?** One- and four-coordinate learned existence lanes were tested through the same lifecycle with noisy coordinates, fixed-rule and protected controls. Both learned split-lane candidates passed all five nominal paired screens and the extended empty-then-write screen. Every-read performance was 99.69%, the same as fixed sign/mean restoration and protected existence, so no learned quality or cost advantage was demonstrated. A jointly refitted 17-dimensional content/existence map failed the nominal joint screen in all five seeds. Stronger corruption remained harmful. Addresses, full writes, score qualification and finite values were supplied: the study did not learn semantic admission or identity binding. [local source: Research/NSS_NBR01_ExistenceContent005_v0.1.zip::NSS_NBR01_ExistenceContent005_v0.1/SUMMARY.json; Research/NSS_WorkspaceSupport_v0.1.zip::.research_review/2026-09-22/experiment_history.md]

**Q006 — can content-based binding and iterative reads follow relations at controlled depth?** Sixteen generated identities, finite records, room/self terminals and four-link training were evaluated to eight-link depth against exact and generic-attention controls. After eight rounds, shared record-hop and gated-hop readers scored 100% for visible known and missing-link cases; they did not solve eight-link chains in four rounds. A four-stage unshared reader also reached 100% on known eight-link chains when its stages were cycled twice. Generic attention controls failed the visible known/missing competence screen. Across 64 events with sigma-0.02 noise on key/value coordinates, both structured learned readers scored 0% on four-link known cases. This supports additional execution of a trained procedure in this setup, not a unique recurrent architecture benefit; noise and identity alignment became the next issue. [local source: Research/NSS_NBR01_BindingDepth006_v0.1.zip::NSS_NBR01_BindingDepth006_v0.1/SUMMARY.json; Research/NSS_WorkspaceSupport_v0.1.zip::.research_review/2026-09-22/experiment_history.md]

**Q007 — can a compatible dictionary or exact restoration repair records without retraining, and distinguish damage from novelty?** Five paired restorers were tested with frozen readers, clean/noisy keys and values, exact-nearest controls and an intrinsic MLP. Dictionary-conditioned and exact-nearest repair restored 100% known and missing-link scores through the nominal 1,024-tick/eight-link condition; the intrinsic MLP failed. At query noise 0.2, repair recovered known recall to 100%, but one missing case turned into a false room answer, leaving UNKNOWN at 99.38%. For unregistered identities, learned repair produced unsupported room answers in 47.81% of cases and nearest repair in 62.81%, versus 0% with no repair. The dictionary condition assumes identity is already in its catalogue. This directly motivated membership-aware repair. [local source: Research/NSS_NBR01_RepairBinding007_v0.1.zip::NSS_NBR01_RepairBinding007_v0.1/SUMMARY.json; Research/NSS_WorkspaceSupport_v0.1.zip::.research_review/2026-09-22/experiment_history.md]

**Q008 — can selective membership gates reject unfamiliar identities while preserving known recovery and writable novelty?** Geometric gate A and learned gate B were paired with nearest repair; parent binders/readers stayed frozen. Ordinary unregistered false acceptance fell to 0.43% and 0.55%, compared with unconditional projection accepting all such inputs. At the altered-known q20 stress, identity accuracy dropped to 70.16% and 66.93%, versus 98.85% without the gates. Nominal state and eight-link known/missing results were 100%, and the ordinary registration lifecycle was 100%, but every selective configuration passed the full registered joint criterion in zero of five seeds. Maintenance every eight ticks passed; every 32 ticks rejected too much recoverable state. A near-duplicate registered identity could still alias a record with a plausible answer. Selectivity reduced one error surface by damaging coverage on another. [local source: Research/NSS_NBR01_MembershipRepair008_v0.1.zip::NSS_NBR01_MembershipRepair008_v0.1/SUMMARY.json; Research/NSS_WorkspaceSupport_v0.1.zip::.research_review/2026-09-22/experiment_history.md]

**Q009 — does exact ownership prevent near-identity overwrite, and can internal recovery use ownership separately from external admission?** The study retained explicit owner/scope/epoch/revision state, frozen learned binders/readers, exact writing/traversal and a separate rule-based cue diagnostic. Exact identity-keyed writes preserved the complete map even for 0.01-close sources and all 7,680 targeted valid transaction comparisons. Near-negative training raised learned binding to 100% complete maps at 0.10 separation, but every learned binder failed at 0.01. An exact conflict guard rejected damaging writes and all useful new writes too. Despite the perfect exact writer, frozen readers reached only about 20% joint correctness for very close sources, destinations or intermediates; exact owner traversal reached 100%. For 1,024 sigma-0.02 noise events with repair every 32 ticks, owner-keyed rehydration plus nearest-value repair and gate A or B scored 100% on tested established-chain and missing-link conditions; unconditional nearest reached 99.38%/100%. No learned pipeline passed the complete near-identity families. This was the strong bounded write/recovery reference and the negative learned near-identity result; NBR-01 remained open pending an explicit closeout decision. ### Context management: public references and freshness (CM-01 and CM-02)

**CM-01 asked whether a bounded, always-resident reference workspace could help the reconstructed reader handle noisy close identities.** It compared the original numerical query (A), a 56-byte ASCII packet (B), the same fields serialized as UTF-8 bytes (C), and public exact lookup; a privileged exact-owner result was kept separate. Five frozen near-reader seeds evaluated 40 held-out records per cell. Close-identity recall rose from 88/200 (44.0%) to 142/200 (71.0%), and ordinary-known recall from 157/200 (78.5%) to 200/200. All five held-out families met the declared gain/control rule. The gain depended on noise: at sigma 0.01 near recall was 71/100 without and 70/100 with the workspace; at sigma 0.2 it rose from 17/100 to 72/100. Exact public lookup was 40/40 on ordinary and close records. B and C agreed on all 7,680 comparisons. This is evidence for reference-assisted query reconstruction in the wrapper, not an always-active native learned-token system, and it does not outperform exact retrieval. The 56 bytes are not 56 model tokens. [local source: Research/NSS_CM01_Execution_v0.2.zip::NSS_CM01_Execution_v0.2/REPORT.md; Research/NSS_Research_Consolidation_v0.1.zip::source_reports/E01__NSS_CM01_Execution_v0.2__REPORT.md]

**CM-02 asked whether a compact manager keeps selections current across chronological changes and whether a string workspace beats an ordinary index.** It compared two 56-byte task packets (W), an ordinary two-entry index with the same fields (I), and an information-ablation arm that discarded selection history (O). Five held-out episode families, five frozen reader seeds and 120 held-out query occasions yielded 391/600 readouts for both W and I versus 307/600 for O: the packet and ordinary index tied exactly. On small-noise queries W/I were worse than O (67.33% vs 74.00%); on sigma-0.2 queries they were better (63.00% vs 28.33%), so the pooled benefit is conditional. Exact pinned retrieval scored 100/120, while following a valid observed revision chain scored 105/120. The pinned policy appropriately abstained after a revision when “current content” was requested; stale reference and deletion were kept distinct. The workspace saved 24 resident graph bytes in this accounting but increased parsing/transient/traffic costs. The study was deterministic information management, not a learned state experiment, compression result or overall efficiency pass. [local source: Research/NSS_CM02_Execution_v0.6.zip::NSS_CM02_Execution_v0.6/REPORT.md; Research/NSS_Research_Consolidation_v0.1.zip::source_reports/E02__NSS_CM02_Execution_v0.6__REPORT.md]

### Learning task state: TS01, failed successors TS02/TS03, and CKA01

**TS01 asked whether a small learned recurrent state can maintain two task checklists across updates, corrections, resets and distractions.** Five new GRUCell(9,64)+Linear(64,9) models, each with 14,985 parameters, carried two 64-float banks (512 state bytes). Supplied code routed each task to its bank, preserved the other bank and filtered labelled no-ops; the learned transition represented checklist progress and next action. Held-out CARRY and PREFIX_REPLAY each scored 600/600 joint answers; ZERO_EVERY_EVENT scored 200/600; a packed exact tracker scored 120/120 on unique occasions. All five fits scored 120/120 on the held-out panel. Carry used 1,612 recurrent transitions versus 4,303 for prefix replay in the specified schedule, but the exact eight-bit progress state fits in one packed byte before metadata, so this is not a compression or system-efficiency win. It was a strong narrow learned temporal-state feasibility result under supplied routing, not learned management-token writing or general task competence. [local source: Research/NSS_Research_Consolidation_v0.1.zip::source_reports/E03__NSS_TS01_Execution_v0.3__REPORT.md]

**TS02-MW01 asked whether a native maintained metadata workspace improves revision-aware derived progress over recurrent-only readers.** Five fits each compared R, larger R+, and RW. RW embedded five field-typed metadata tokens per task into the recurrent update; deterministic code still wrote observable metadata and routed tasks, while heads read recurrent state alone. Held-out joint progress/action scores were R 507/600 (84.50%), R+ 496/600 (82.67%), RW 526/600 (87.67%). However, only three of five RW fits met the 90% floor, below the required four, and the known-evidence repair subset tied R+ at 79/125. RW also made 75 stale or unsupported revision claims out of 1,200 and 17 wrong-DONE assertions in 100 designated assessments, against a zero-allowance rule. This was a failed reliability/safety screen. It suggests a small pooled workspace advantage on this grid but does not isolate workspace representation from model capacity, establish learned writing, or support a native workspace adoption. [local source: Research/NSS_Research_Consolidation_v0.1.zip::source_reports/E04__NSS_TS02_MW01_Execution_v0.2__REPORT.md; Research/NSS_Research_Consolidation_v0.1.zip::source_receipts/R04__NSS_TS02_MW01_Execution_v0.2_Delivery.json]

**TS03-CR01 asked whether better exposure to joint metadata/revision combinations repairs TS02’s derived-progress and binding errors.** It crossed two curricula (C0/C1) with R, larger R+ and RW in four paired fits per cell; all 24 main models received 800 updates, with only step 800 scored. C1 redistributed whole right-source histories to expose a previously absent joint revision combination while preserving marginal histories; it also changed other joint exposure and was not just one added tuple. The designated primary repair panel contained 144 readouts per cell. R rose from 37/144 to 49/144 joint correctness; R+ from 20/144 to 35/144; RW fell from 58/144 to 51/144 even though RW binding remained 144/144. C1-RW reached only 34–36/80 per fit against a required 72/80 floor, made 96 invalid revision claims in 640 group predictions and six designated wrong-DONE assertions in 128 readouts. On reserved asymmetric combinations, it scored 16/48 joint and 0/48 correct binding pairs. The primary coverage, interaction, reliability and final-advantage screens all failed. Since even the recurrent-only controls had serious failures and the state/head were jointly trained, this did not identify one unique fault or prove workspace conditioning can never help. [local source: Research/NSS_Research_Consolidation_v0.1.zip::source_reports/E05__NSS_TS03_CR01_Execution_v0.4__REPORT.md; Research/NSS_Research_Consolidation_v0.1.zip::source_receipts/R05__NSS_TS03_CR01_Execution_v0.4_Delivery.json]

**CKA01 asked whether later training damaged competence or whether the failed TS03 results reflected incomplete fitting and weak chronological transfer.** It retrospectively scored 165 saved checkpoints across 29 fits (five TS01 fits and 24 TS03 fits); it ran no fit, optimizer update or backward pass. TS01 rose from 363/600 at update 250 to 600/600 by update 1,000 and stayed there through update 2,000; a new same-recipe panel became uniformly correct at update 1,250. This does not support “overtraining” as the main TS01 failure explanation. TS03 C1-RW on original chronological stress queries rose from 83/320 at update 200 to 143/320 at 600, then slipped to 139/320 at 800. No fit exceeded 39/80 on that stress set. #### ELI5: what happened?

Imagine a checklist with eight items. The learner is supposed to name the first item still unfinished, or say “done” only after every item is complete. It correctly says item 5 is next. Then it is told to mark item 1 complete again, do nothing twice, and mark item 3 complete again. Those commands leave the checklist exactly as it was: item 5 is still pending. But the learner starts saying “done,” and becomes more confident in that wrong answer each time. Only later, when item 5 is actually completed, is “done” correct.

The model's weights did not change during this sequence. So this particular error is not evidence that learning updates erased an earlier skill. It is a fixed-weight failure to preserve the task's meaning through redundant, meaning-preserving events. The hidden numbers inside the model do not need to stay identical from event to event; the requirement is that the answer stay correct when the checklist's meaning has not changed. [local source: `Research/NSS_NBR02_SURFACE01_Execution_v0.1.zip::REPORT.md` §4, event table and final paragraph]

#### Question, method, and reported result

The useful question left by the stop is: **what makes a pending item remain correctly represented and read out while other items are resolved and later events leave the checklist unchanged?** The executed instrument was a 26-input GRU cell with 64 hidden units and a nine-class linear head (18,249 parameters). It received event tokens for SET, CLEAR, RESET, NOOP and a second task's cue; it was not given the current checklist mask, answer, or valid-action set. The harness provided routing, initial state and independent labels. Task A required one of eight flags (lowest unfinished) or DONE. **Training** streams were 32 events, with four event-family types. A secondary cue task was fit through the same head. Four parents (seeds 4101–4104) each received 2,000 Adam updates under the same declared recipe. Admission tested 256 histories per parent, with 32 scored suffix events after a prefix interrupted at event 8 or 24 (40- or 56-event complete histories). Requirements were at least 99% suffix-event accuracy, 95% wholly correct continuations, zero false-DONE, and all eight secondary answers with probability at least 0.80. [local source: `Research/NSS_NBR02_SURFACE01_Execution_v0.1.zip::REPORT.md` §§2–3]

Three parents passed their own admission panels perfectly. Parent 4103 got 8,186 of 8,192 scored suffix events right and 255 of 256 histories wholly right, but made six false-DONE predictions in one idempotent history. Its event and full-history accuracy thresholds passed; the strict zero-false-DONE criterion alone failed. The catalog's local recount matched all 16 retained parent-by-family rows: only 4103/idempotent had errors (2,042/2,048 events correct, 63/64 histories correct, six false DONE). The descriptive total was 32,762/32,768 events and 1,023/1,024 histories. Because every parent had to pass, the cohort stopped. The other three parents were not tested on 4103's exact failing history, so their perfect panels do not show that they would handle this same sequence. Pooled scores are descriptive, not a substitute for the per-parent gate or additional independent fits. [local source: `Research/NSS_NBR02_SURFACE01_Execution_v0.1.zip::analysis/parent_family_results.csv`; `evidence/admission_4101.npz`–`admission_4104.npz`; catalog retained-logit recount]

The failing history reached exact mask 223 at event 16, after SET(0); item 5 was then the lowest remaining unfinished item. Repeating SET(1), doing two NOOPs, and repeating SET(3) left mask 223 unchanged, yet events 17–22 were classified DONE. The assigned softmax score for DONE rose from 47.8078% to 99.4094%. At event 23, SET(5) actually completed the checklist; predicting DONE was then correct. That later correct answer is not recovery evidence. The report says these logits came from a fixed-weight rollout. [local source: `Research/NSS_NBR02_SURFACE01_Execution_v0.1.zip::REPORT.md` §4, event-by-event table]

#### What this supports and what remains open

The false completion happened at events 17–22, within the same 32-event sequence length used for training. Thus “it only failed because this history ran longer than training” does not explain this observation. The report also says all 256 masks appeared in training, and mask 223 appeared in 49,823 training events, including 1,389 same-mask SET(1) transitions. That contradicts the simple claim that this symbolic mask or immediate idempotent transition was wholly absent. It does **not** prove the model learned every relevant history-dependent distinction: exposure counts are not counts of independent, equivalent trajectories. [local source: `Research/NSS_NBR02_SURFACE01_Execution_v0.1.zip::REPORT.md` §4, “What the retained evidence rules out”]

The report's 160-stream training diagnostic had 5,115/5,120 correct events and 159/160 perfect streams, with no false DONE. That is a sample of actual training streams, not the full training corpus. It leaves ordinary fitting/optimization weakness plausible alongside limitations of the objective, representation or capacity. Current findings do not isolate one of those causes. Nor does a wrong output prove that pending-item information vanished from the hidden state: information might remain encoded but the original output head could misread it. A held-out per-flag decoder over the supplied hidden-state arrays can test whether the flag is readily decodable; a positive result would still not prove what caused the original head's error, while a negative result would not prove that no decoder could recover it. [local source: `Research/NSS_NBR02_SURFACE01_Execution_v0.1.zip::evidence/admission_4103.npz`]

The report states that its full-trajectory float32 reference checks, fresh-process panel replay, 33 model-tensor hash checks and eight injected-fault detectors passed under the predeclared tests. Catalog verified archive members against the manifest; it did not recompute those model-tensor hashes. These are useful reported instrumentation and reproducibility checks, not fresh scientific verification. They do not repair the earlier STATEBRIDGE numerical gate, which RESEARCH_STATE still lists as failed, and they do not turn this review into an independent replication. [local source: `Research/NSS_NBR02_SURFACE01_Execution_v0.1.zip::REPORT.md` §5 and `checkpoints/`; `RESEARCH_STATE.md`, “Earlier STATEBRIDGE01 v0.2”]

It would be a mistake to infer that all recurrent hidden coordinates should stay constant on NOOP or repeated SET. Only semantic/output invariance is required. It would also be premature to claim a specific topology, lower learning rate, longer training, tri-core split or adaptive surface will fix the issue. The result is one connected six-event failure in one history from one of four fits. The main planned comparison was never reached. The status remains consistent with the research board: NBR-02 is active but paused; Chat owns future implementation and experiments, while this review only proposes analysis. [local source: `RESEARCH_STATE.md`, “Current position” and “Architecture”; `RESEARCH_PLAN.md`, “Research division after this pass”]

## Architecture direction and assessment

### Historical roles and proposed lifecycle

The historical operating boundary remains a frozen participating Primary, a task and learning Secondary, a predictive and reflective Tertiary that cannot execute concurrently with the Secondary, and a separate frozen Validator that advises on evidence but does not grant permission or veto outputs. This is a functional and scheduling boundary preserved for comparability. It does not prove that NSS needs four separately parameterized models.

The later architecture whiteboard proposes a different lifecycle view: keep accepted serving weights stable during ordinary use, allow current task state and source-backed evidence to change, train a candidate during a bounded low-usage period, test new and old abilities, and publish only a qualified revision. That lifecycle is a candidate design, not an adopted change to the frozen role contract and not a demonstrated whole NSS system. It keeps the serving model from receiving a hidden update every time a user corrects a fact while allowing current evidence to be used immediately through a governed record or retrieval path.

Keep three questions separate when evaluating the architecture. First, can the system answer with current evidence? Second, can a model learn a reusable skill from experience? Third, can the system safely change the serving revision and continue unfinished work? Retrieval can solve the first question without proving the second; successful training can solve neither the third nor the question of full-system value by itself.



### Functional design and object lifetimes

The most useful design contribution is the separation of functions and state lifetimes. The proposed harness manages evidence, task boundaries, model versions, tools and permissions around one or more learned backbones. The harness can make an exact operation reliable; that reliability must not be reported as a neural model capability. Conversely, a learned model's confidence does not establish permission, source truth or successful publication.

| Object | Purpose | Lifetime and write rule |
|---|---|---|
| Accepted model parameters | Reusable learned competence | Stable within a serving generation; change only through a versioned candidate and an independent qualification decision. |
| Task working state | Unfinished computation and event continuity | Owned by one task and model revision. It changes during work and must be resumed, migrated or rebuilt under a tested rule. |
| Exact evidence | Facts, corrections, outcomes, source excerpts and provenance | Updated when a legitimate source changes. Preserve the source and its scope; do not silently substitute a summary. |
| Management workspace | Current goal, scope, model and evidence versions, open checks and task references | A bounded working view over durable records, not a substitute for the full archive. |
| Optional fast learned memory | Temporary learned associations beyond explicit evidence | A separately declared adaptive surface with its own scope, interference, reset and retention tests. Calling it a cache does not exempt it from learning rules. |
| Repair-policy state | Decisions about which repairs to apply and their observed effects | Persisted and versioned separately from task scratch state; do not change it at the same time as the learner and evaluator when measuring one effect. |
| Training and eligibility state | Optimizer, gradients, event traces, random state and job cursor | Bound to a specific training job and checkpoint. Count it in storage and resume behavior. |
| Appraisal or priority state | Significance, uncertainty and investigation priority | Must not be used as evidence, truth or authorization. Its effects need a no-appraisal control. |

A small management packet may point to the task, evidence revisions, model version, pending feedback, unresolved work and last fully accounted cutoff. It is an index into evidence, not a certificate that the evidence is complete. Exact records and provenance remain available for rechecking.

### Candidate model topologies

The review supports keeping the role contract while making physical model boundaries contestable. The functions may be distinct even if the implementation shares some parameters or infrastructure. No current result shows that separate weight sets outperform a shared model at equal capability, information, latency and total cost. Sharing also does not create independent review: components with common training, representations or failure modes may repeat the same error.

| Candidate | What it keeps or changes | Evidence needed before choosing it |
|---|---|---|
| Current-role extension | Keep Primary, alternating Secondary and Tertiary, and advisory Validator; improve supported interfaces, evidence layout and maintenance. | Show that auxiliaries add end-to-end value beyond the Primary and ordinary retrieval/search, at their complete memory and runtime cost. |
| Native-state tri-core | Keep the same roles and Secondary/Tertiary alternation, but train a recurrent or hybrid Primary and explicit state-native interfaces. | Demonstrate held-out depth, robust state renewal, language competence, complete cost and migration against a competent current baseline. “Start from scratch” can mean a new mathematical architecture, random initialization, or a software-stack rewrite. Those choices do not imply one another. A new neural candidate can reuse the existing archive, evaluation, scheduling, and generic Forge tooling. Rewriting the software harness has no automatic cognitive benefit. A simple rule or exact retrieval remains a valid control when it solves the declared task more reliably or cheaply.

### Proposed wake consolidation and renewal lifecycle

The whiteboard lifecycle is best treated as a sequence of versioned transactions, not as an assertion that sleep has already improved the system.

1. **Serve from an accepted revision.** Keep the current model parameters unchanged during the serving epoch. Tasks use task-local state and may consult current, scoped evidence. Record which model and source revisions support each action.
2. **Record new information immediately.** Store a correction or new fact with provenance and scope. Identify unfinished tasks that depend on superseded information. Evidence freshness and weight freshness are different contracts: an old model revision must not silently ignore a current correction.
3. **Freeze a cutoff.** At a scheduled low-usage boundary, record the experience cutoff, pending feedback, incomplete tasks and unresolved obligations. Do not move the cutoff in the middle of review or mark missing information as processed.
4. **Build a lawful candidate curriculum.** Select verified new experience, old skills, rare exceptions and known failure cases. Preserve source identity and keep training, development and confirmation partitions separate. Generated or imagined material remains labeled hypothetical until an independent source establishes it.
5. **Train a candidate under a finite budget.** Pin the parent, eligible examples, trainable surfaces, seeds, optimizer, random-state policy, time and storage limits. Save interruption and unknown-completion state. Preparation, teacher calls, practice generation, replay and evaluation consume the same budget as gradient updates.
6. **Evaluate gain and collateral effects.** Measure acquisition, old behavior, task resumption, source/evidence use, defect detection, cost and rejected or deferred useful updates. Keep the evaluator version and acceptance criteria fixed. A failed candidate stays failed; do not relax gates after observing its score.
7. **Reconstruct and test actual tasks.** Build new task state from sufficient records or use an explicitly qualified migration. Test continuation while the compatible old revision and state still exist. Replaying a transcript is not necessarily reconstructing the internal computation that produced the live state.
8. **Publish with rollback or defer.** Bind the candidate, evidence cutoff, evaluation receipt, state schema and publication event. Retain a tested fallback. If a task cannot be resumed adequately, preserve it on a compatible version or defer rather than discarding it to make a clean start.

“Sleep” is a proposed low-usage window, not a biological claim or measured runtime. The earlier one-to-two-hour estimate is an owner-suggested cap, not an observed completion time. Candidate preparation, training, review, checkpointing, migration and backlog all need measured costs.

### Resume and evaluation contract

A resume record should bind task identity and scope, the model revision, source/evidence cutoff, completed actions, invalidated actions, tool artifacts, side-effect receipts, outstanding dependencies, pending feedback and the references needed to check completeness. Test that record while the old state remains available. A summary may be concise, but it cannot be presumed complete because it reads well.

Renewing temporary state is not the same as renewing identity, preserving personal facts or repairing competence. Resetting to an empty vector removes only temporary constraints; it loses any decisive information that existed only in that vector. Rebuilding from history depends on a capable current model and adequate history. Changing weights may improve a skill but does not automatically reconstruct an unfinished task. Never replay an old optimizer step or stale gradient as though it were merely replaying history.

Evaluation coverage should grow by building on old obligations. A later suite rebuild may reorganize duplicate machinery only after separately checking both the learner and the test system. A strong learner passing a new suite does not show that the suite still catches old defects. Keep stable regression anchors, rotating older skills, targeted risk checks and a genuinely reserved confirmation channel distinct. When a failed holdout becomes a repair example, label it as training or development material from then on.

The evaluator needs evaluation too. A vulnerability-focused panel is useful as a warning channel, but it should not be used as the representative overall-impact estimate. EVAL01 showed both sides of this tradeoff: the targeted policy admitted fewer out-of-envelope changes in its snapshot set while deferring more acceptable offers. Record harmful approvals, useful adaptations blocked or delayed, appropriate deferrals and unknown cases. No neural Validator becomes an oracle or permission authority through agreement with the Primary.

Bound version residency and the deferred-update backlog. Stable serving avoids abrupt parameter change but can delay reusable learning and leave old revisions using fresh information through context. The design needs a limit on resident revisions, checkpoint and rollback storage, quiet-period compute, task reload latency and corrections waiting for consolidation. If new obligations arrive faster than successful update cycles resolve them, individual cycle success does not establish a sustainable lifecycle.

### Codex assessment

The strongest part of the proposal is its explicit separation of accepted parameters, live task state, exact evidence, candidate learning, review and publication. This is a sensible control surface for a companion that must use current facts without changing its learned parameters for each fact. The proposal also chooses the right outcome unit: a complete learning and resumption cycle, including new gain, old-capability impact, disruption, recovery cost and later behavior. It is stronger than a single final score or a policy that preserves stability by refusing all useful changes.

Three limits determine the current assessment. First, no full NSS lifecycle comparison shows that the tri-core's complexity earns its cost. The work has not compared the production role topology with equal-information extra compute or a shared-backbone challenger. Second, the resume and evaluation transactions remain proposals; current study harnesses do not establish system-scale task recovery, atomic publication or reliable operation across model revisions. Third, existing cost accounting is incomplete. Do not claim that recurrent state, sparse updates, low-bit codes or a quiet-period schedule is cheaper until measured peak and retained memory, preparation, inference, replay, evaluation and migration costs are included.

The evidence contains meaningful bounded ingredients. Exact owner-keyed records and retrieval solve some narrow bookkeeping tasks well. TS01 shows a small learned checklist state can carry progress when routing and no-op filtering are supplied. DC01 demonstrates a bounded identified delayed-feedback learner. MICROMACRO01 qualifies one narrowly scoped repair cycle under its prospective three-point mean-recovery contract. TEACH01 reports a modest reduction in omitted-old-record loss from a fixed teacher surrogate. These results justify retaining the mechanisms as candidates and controls; they do not qualify a trained Tertiary, an always-active Primary scheduler, or a complete self-maintaining companion.

The negative results are equally decisive. Close-identity learned readers remain weak even alongside perfect exact writers. TS02 and TS03 show revision and progress errors despite metadata interfaces; correct binding alone does not guarantee correct derived behavior. PROTECT01 and REHEARSE01 stopped at development admission, while REV01 and TIMING01 expose old-confidence or transient-loss costs. STATEBRIDGE directly demonstrates why model revision and task state must be tested together. The positive outcomes cannot be averaged with the failures into a broad architectural win because their tasks, denominators and gates differ.

On current evidence, keep stable serving, source-backed live evidence and bounded candidate learning as a coherent design proposal. Preserve the historical frozen role boundary until an explicit comparison answers whether the roles require distinct parameter sets. First diagnose SURFACE01 baseline semantic stability, then return to isolating STATEBRIDGE-style update-surface effects once a competent baseline exists. Do not select a product topology from biological resemblance, parameter counts, one synthetic task or a fluent demonstration.

SURFACE01 sharpens this assessment: **stable parameters do not guarantee stable task meaning**. Three parents passed their own panels, so this is not a broad inability to learn the checklist. One connected fixed-weight failure is enough to reject the declared cohort gate, but not enough to choose capacity, optimization, representation or readout as its cause. A pending flag may remain encoded even when the head says DONE. Compare shared histories and controlled semantic variants before selecting a new topology. ### Low bit learning state and its actual cost

Eligibility traces are temporary numerical state that links earlier activity to a later learning signal. They are one possible way to address delayed credit in recurrent learning. G12 asks whether traces and updates can retain useful information at one or two bits. No retained NSS experiment has answered that question: the current adaptive studies use ordinary floating-point state. Low-bit storage remains a proposal.

The raw arithmetic is attractive but narrow. One billion float32 values require 4,000,000,000 bytes, or 4 GB decimal (about 3.725 GiB). If each value were actually packed, the value array alone would require 250 MB at two bits, 125 MB at one bit, 500 MB at four bits, 1 GB at eight bits, or 2 GB at sixteen bits. For fifty billion values, those respective sizes are 12.5 GB, 6.25 GB, 25 GB, 50 GB and 100 GB. These are arithmetic examples, not measured NSS requirements, implemented tensors or total training memory.

The two-bit number omits block scales, indices, timestamps, sign or age channels, accumulators, optimizer moments, gradients, activations, RNG state, weights, replay data, alignment, framework overhead, copies and temporary decoding buffers. A two-bit value per entry with a 16-bit scale every 128 entries averages 2.125 bits per value, about 265.625 MB for one billion entries before the other costs. Two value bits plus a 32-bit index cost 34 bits per active sparse entry; that representation beats a dense two-bit value array only below about 5.88 percent occupancy before time and structure overhead. A sparse live set can become dense when traces persist. These costs must be measured in the actual implementation, including its temporary peak.

Precision is a set of separate design decisions. Serving weights, recurrent working state, eligibility traces, learning signals, optimizer state, master or residual accumulators, and exact records have different roles. A ternary or two-bit inference weight format does not imply two-bit gradients or traces. The cited low-bit optimizer work concerns tested optimizer moments; it does not establish an adequate eligibility-trace algorithm. Storing a packed representation is also different from computing natively on packed values. A uint8 or Boolean tensor may still take one byte per element, and a dequantize-update-requantize loop may consume higher-precision workspace.

The failure modes are concrete. With representable trace levels {-1, 0, 1}, repeated rounding of 0.99 leaves a trace at 1 forever; repeated 0.01 additions to zero can round away forever. The exact scalar decay `e[t+1] = lambda * e[t]` gives `0.99^100 = 0.366032...`, whereas those rounded examples do not preserve the decay or accumulation. A signed trace also needs to retain polarity; one bit cannot encode positive, zero and negative as distinct values. Two bits offer only four states and very coarse magnitude and age information.

Candidate mechanisms include block quantization with local wider updates, stochastic decay or rounding, bounded residual or hysteretic accumulation, sparse traces with aging, factored approximations, and storing enough event history to recompute traces when feedback arrives. Each moves cost elsewhere: scales and random state, variance and more samples, residual memory, indices and timestamps, approximation error, recomputation time, or compatible historical weights and activations. Stochastic unbiasedness at one operation does not guarantee unbiased downstream learning through nonlinear dynamics.

The credible next step, when the owner resumes science, is a trace-specific microbenchmark against a float32 reference over declared decay, accumulation, sign-change, delay and reversal cases. Compare 16-, 8-, 4- and 2-bit stored state under the same task, traces and budget; treat a one-bit method as a deliberately different discrete or probabilistic learner. Measure packed bytes, temporary peak, runtime, energy, RNG-resume correctness and task-level delayed-credit performance. Do not shrink the research program to a small readout merely because full adaptation is expensive; first identify which surface needs updating and which traces the chosen rule mathematically requires.

### How to use these sources

The literature serves three roles: candidate computational mechanisms and controls; observations or models in specific nervous systems; and implementation guidance on model storage and memory. None is direct evidence that an NSS study passed, a proposed module exists in code, or the full architecture works. NSS results remain in the reports, protocols, and archives indexed in `RESEARCH_INDEX.md`.

The fruit-fly work cautions against simple size analogies. FlyWire is an adult female *Drosophila* wiring map, not a complete record of effective strengths, dynamic states, learning rules, modulation, sensory history, or behavior. Separate fly studies add task-specific models and interventions. The safe inference is methodological: circuit structure can constrain hypotheses, and scoped models can make testable predictions. A biological neuron is not one artificial parameter, a synapse is not one scalar weight, and connectome cell counts cannot be directly compared with language-model parameter counts. A fly central-brain model of selected sensorimotor transformations is not a functioning digital fly.

Keep human and other-mammal studies distinct. Gidon et al. measured dendritic activity in human cortical tissue ex vivo; Hassabis et al. studied people with hippocampal amnesia; Liu et al. studied replay in people. Murray et al. analyzed macaque working-memory recordings, and the sleep papers concern mice. These results motivate questions about population codes and replay but are not human sleep experiments or NSS schedules. Biological sleep does not establish that an agent should “dream,” that synthetic replay preserves experience, or that one subsystem should train another overnight.

Low-bit citations support experiments, not claims that a whole learner operates at its parameter tensor’s nominal bitwidth. Moments, eligibility traces, gradients, master values, scales, indices, RNG, activations, temporary buffers, and KV caches have distinct costs and error dynamics. Two-bit optimizer results do not establish two-bit trace learning. BitNet’s “1.58-bit” label describes ternary weights; its official release separates packed inference weights from BF16 training weights. Measure actual bytes and retain a higher-precision reference.

It is a memory mechanism comparator, not proof of reliable autobiographical recall or safe revision.

5. **C-R2.** Dehghani et al. (2018; ICLR 2019), [“Universal Transformers”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03819), arXiv:1807.03819. It is relevant to testing learned recurrent depth; repeated refinement is not the same variable as retaining state across real-world episodes.

6. **C-R3.** Dai et al. (2019; ACL 2019), [“Transformer-XL: Attentive Language Models Beyond a Fixed-Length Context”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.02860), arXiv:1901.02860. 7. **C-R4.** Geiping et al. (2025), [“Scaling up Test-Time Compute with Latent Reasoning: A Recurrent Depth Approach”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05171), cited arXiv version 2502.05171; publication status not checked. The paper studies iterative latent computation at inference. NSS should compare added within-problem computation separately from elapsed-event state and parameter learning; the work does not establish lifelong memory.

#### Continual learning, repair, replay, and evaluation

15. **A-R12.** Shin et al. (2017), [“Continual Learning with Deep Generative Replay”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08690), arXiv:1705.08690. 16. **A-R13.** Wang et al. (2018), [“Dataset Distillation”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.10959), arXiv:1811.10959. 17. **A-R14.** Hinton, Vinyals, and Dean (2015), [“Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531), arXiv:1503.02531. ### Verification and transfer boundary

The online check confirmed the identity and limited scope of the selected sources from publisher proceedings, primary journals, arXiv records, PubMed/PMC, eLife, and official project documentation. A small number of primary pages were inaccessible to this fetcher: the Science and Cell pages returned 403, and one PNAS DOI redirected to a cookie-required page. Those are recorded as access limits, not as evidence against the citation. No article was downloaded to the workspace, and no cited model, code, simulation, or benchmark was run.

For the Chat handoff, cite the biology as design inspiration and causal hypotheses, not as architectural authority. Keep exact source observations and prior failures outside model summaries. Preserve the numerical distinction between parameter count, neuron count, synapse count, synaptic state, model memory, and total training footprint. Keep human results separate from macaque and mouse results, and keep study-specific evidence separate from NSS’s own measured outcomes. `NETWORK_ACCESS.json` records the primary URLs, exact search queries, outcomes, and available retrieval-time precision for this verification pass.
